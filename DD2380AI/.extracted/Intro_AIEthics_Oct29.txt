             Mutual Shaping of Technology & Society
             The prevalent world view in robotics and AI has traditionally been technological
             determinism, which can be summarised with the saying:


                             “Science finds ~ industry applies ~ man conforms”


             Typical signs of technological determinism:
             • Technological advancement ≡ societal success
             • Focus on (justifying) technological fixes, not understanding the problem
             • Design/application choices being made only by ‘technical’ experts

             Mutual Shaping of Technology & Society


             TD is a pretty arrogant and narrow viewpoint:
             “In their emphasis on technological advancement as the determinant of
             societal success, roboticists define the societal issues they wish to solve
             ‘not [as] social, political, cultural, historical problems, but [as] biotechnological
             problems that call for biotechnological solutions’. Society, in turn, is displayed
             as a passive receptor of the products of visioning and negotiation performed by

             TD neglects human agency and ignores social context

             [2] Involving Society in Technology Design


             Including users at the early stages of design is important for “developing socially
             robust, rather than merely acceptable, robotic technologies.”


             Stakeholders (people who are going to be directly impacted by the tech. being
             developed, and who have tacit knowledge of the application domain) should have
             opportunities to influence its design, the kind of applications to be developed, how
             such tech. will be used and what their broader social meaning will be.


             Participatory design, co-design: focus groups, interviews, workshops,
             interdisciplinary hackathons, crowdsourcing…

Final Recap


Technological determinism has driven a lot of tech. development, and likely
resulted in a lot the problematic issues we see know (e.g. racism, sexism in AI).


A more accurate view of tech in the world can be achieved by taking a mutual
shaping view. Actively pursuing mutual shaping means to (i) properly situate tech
into our lives and social systems and (ii) invite society into tech design. Both of
which leads to more effective tech.


Emerging ethical standards build on this provide practical guidance for ethical AI
development and tangible ways to assess and address ethical risk.

AI can impact several aspects of our daily lives, such as
• Labour
• Surveillance & privacy
• Sustainability
• Decision-making (credit-score attribution, health diagnosis, recommendations, …)
